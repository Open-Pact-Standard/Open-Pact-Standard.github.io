The License

Fair source is a deliberate middle ground.

Open-source software is free for everyone — including large companies that build billion-dollar products without paying the developers who made it possible. OPL changes that equation while keeping the source open for everyone else.

🏠

Personal use — free

Study, modify, fork, and share. Education, research, evaluation, personal projects, and internal non-revenue tooling. No payment required. No permission needed.

💰

Commercial use — paid

Hosted services, paid products, SaaS offerings, and distributing derivatives for commercial purposes. Payment goes to the Maintainer per their published Standard Terms.

⚖️

Maintainer — full control

You set the price, the payment mechanism, and the scope. Smart contract, Stripe, GitHub Sponsors, bank wire, or “email me.” You can change terms with 90 days’ notice.

🔒

Not open source — by design

OPL restricts commercial use unless the Maintainer publishes Standard Terms. It disciplines commercial use while keeping the source public and readable. That’s the trade-off, and it’s intentional.

The software you rely on deserves to be sustainable.

OPL is a private license — a contract between the Maintainer and the user, resolved under the jurisdiction the Maintainer declares. No middleman, no platform, no cut.

Built-In Protections

Designed for real-world use.

OPL v1.4 includes provisions other fair-source licenses don’t — so adoption is safe and the source is never orphaned.

📤

Automatic Apache Conversion

If the Maintainer is unreachable for 36 consecutive months, OPL v1.4 grants Apache License 2.0 to every recipient automatically — no actor, successor, or registry required. A Maintainer may optionally name a Designated Successor in NOTICE solely to record the conversion. No orphaned code.

📅

Optional Scheduled Open-Sourcing (DOSP)

Want your source to become Apache 2.0 on a set date? Opt into Version-Based Delayed Open-Source Publication (DOSP) in NOTICE — recommended 24–60 months. Silent on it? No conversion. The Maintainer decides.

💨

90-Day Wind-Down

If the Maintainer revokes commercial use or materially changes Standard Terms, existing commercial users get a 90-day wind-down period. Previous terms continue to apply during the transition.

🤖

AI Training Addendum (Opt-In)

Want to restrict AI training on your code? OPL-AI provides canary tokens and attestation — all opt-in via a single line in NOTICE. Default: AI training is permitted.

📃

Commercial Terms File

Pin exact pricing, payment, and scope in a per-Version, immutable COMMERCIAL_TERMS.md referenced from NOTICE — a predictable, lawyer-reviewable contract independent of the Standard Terms URL.

📋

Zero Infrastructure Required

No registry. No smart contracts. No on-chain fee collection. No framework. Copy the license, create a NOTICE file, and you’re done.

Version History

What changed in v1.4.

OPL v1.4 is an adoption-focused revision of the published v1.3.1. Four changes matter.

1

Optional Scheduled Open-Sourcing (DOSP)

New §5.1 lets a Maintainer opt into Version-Based Delayed Open-Source Publication: a declared DOSP date after which each Version’s source converts to Apache 2.0. OPL does not force it — a silent NOTICE means no scheduled conversion.

2

Functional-Equivalent Restriction Removed

The old §3.6 restriction is gone. OPL no longer restricts independently-developed, functionally-similar works — actual copying stays protected by copyright law. Removes the “code taint” anxiety that discouraged adoption.

3

Commercial Terms File & Extended NOTICE

NOTICE gains two optional fields: a DOSP period (§5.1) and a per-Version immutable COMMERCIAL_TERMS.md pinning exact pricing, payment, and scope.

4

Robust Abandonment

§5 now grants Apache-2.0 automatically on abandonment — no actor, registry, or successor required. A Maintainer may name a Designated Successor in NOTICE to merely record the conversion, never gate it.

Get Started

Adopt OPL in five minutes.

No registry, no framework, no on-chain transactions. Five steps and you’re done.

One command (recommended)

Skip the manual steps — the hardened opl_adopt spine runs the whole thing and prints a final VERDICT:

python3 opl_adopt.py --maintainer "You <you@example.com>" \
  --jurisdiction "United States" \
  --terms-url "https://example.com/standard-terms"

Available from the Studio workbench, the CLI, or any agent harness via MCP (adopt-1cmd). The five steps below are the manual equivalent if you prefer to do it by hand.

1

Copy the License

Add LICENSE.md to your repository root.

2

Create NOTICE

Copy NOTICE.example to NOTICE. Fill in the Maintainer name, contact, jurisdiction, and Standard Terms URL.

3

Publish Standard Terms

Create a page at your declared URL with pricing, payment mechanism, and scope — Stripe, smart contract, or “email me.”

4

Add SPDX Identifier

Add SPDX-License-Identifier: OPL-1.4 to each source file header.

5

Update Manifest

Reference the license in pyproject.toml, Cargo.toml, or package.json.

After you run it — what you have & what to do

The opl_adopt command leaves your repo with four artifacts. There is no license key, token, or account to store — you are the licensor by publishing these files.

  • 1. LICENSE — the canonical Open-Pact License v1.4 text.
  • 2. NOTICE — names you as Maintainer, your jurisdiction, your Standard Terms URL, and your OPL-AI choice.
  • 3. SPDX headers — every source file now carries SPDX-License-Identifier: OPL-1.4.
  • 4. Manifests — package files set license = "OPL-1.4".

Your three next steps:

  • Publish your Standard Terms page at the URL you passed. Generate a starter page with opl_init.py --terms-template (a static HTML page with your pricing is enough).
  • Commit the four artifacts above.
  • (Optional) tag a release noting “now under OPL-1.4.”
For Users of OPL Software

Using software under the Open-Pact License

OPL is a two-party license: it protects the maker and gives you, the user, clear rights. If you found a project with SPDX-License-Identifier: OPL-1.4 in its files, here is what that means for you.

You may, for free

  • Use it for personal projects, education, and research — no payment, no account, no key.
  • Read, modify, and share the source (it stays public and readable by design).
  • Fork it and build on it for non-commercial purposes.

Commercial use requires payment

  • Using it to make money requires the Maintainer's permission via their Standard Terms.
  • Find the terms at the Standard Terms URL printed in the project's NOTICE file — that page shows pricing and how to pay.
  • You choose how to comply: pay, or keep your use non-commercial.

You are protected

  • If the Maintainer goes silent for 36 consecutive months (configurable in NOTICE), the work automatically converts to Apache-2.0 — no registry, no successor needed.
  • If NOTICE says OPL-AI: opted in, do not train AI models on the code.
  • If the Maintainer asks about your licensing, reply within 60 days.

No license key to store, no sign-up. The NOTICE + LICENSE files in the repo are the contract. Unsure of your obligations on a specific repo? Run opl_check.py --as-user <repo> to see them in plain language. Respect the commercial terms and you are in the clear.

Questions

Frequently asked.

Is OPL “open source”?
No. OPL restricts commercial use unless the Maintainer publishes Standard Terms. This disqualifies it from the Open Source Initiative’s definition. OPL is fair source — a deliberate middle ground between proprietary and open source.
What happens if the Maintainer disappears?
After 36 consecutive months of unreachability (configurable from 12–60 months in NOTICE), OPL v1.4 automatically grants Apache License 2.0 to every recipient — no actor, successor, or registry is required. The Maintainer may optionally name a Designated Successor in NOTICE to record the conversion. No orphaned code.
Can I use OPL for an open-source project?
If you want truly open source (OSI-approved), use Apache 2.0, MIT, or GPL. OPL is for Maintainers who want to keep the source public and readable while reserving the right to charge for commercial use.
What does the Maintainer have to do?
Maintain a NOTICE file with your contact and Standard Terms URL. Respond to licensing inquiries within 60 days. You choose the pricing, the payment mechanism, and the scope.
Does OPL restrict AI training?
By default, no. The OPL-AI addendum is opt-in. If the Maintainer declares OPL-AI: opted in. in NOTICE, AI training is restricted, using canary tokens and attestation mechanisms.
Who enforces the license?
The Maintainer, through standard contract enforcement. OPL is a private license — a contract between the Maintainer and the user. Disputes are resolved under the jurisdiction declared in NOTICE.
